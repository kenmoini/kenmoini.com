> Easy local AI for people with money to burn

I have given into the latest craze - and no, I'm not talking about Stanley cups.  I'm talking about AI.

To be fair, I've done AI/ML things for years from using the Hortonworks/Cloudera stacks for traditional ML with regression models, YOLO/Darknet things for Computer Vision, and various other tasks where AI/ML has fit a good use case.  I've helped customers get up to speed for things like protection and safety analysis, quality assurance, trend prediction, and more.

But all that is different now - no one cares about any of that, it's all about chat bots and transcriptions these days!  Jokes aside, there's certainly some use to modern AI and SLMs/LLMs but it's mostly been a practice of throwing spaghetti at the wall and hoping that no one negotiates with your bot for a free car.

AI/ML is hard - building models is not for the faint of heart.  I've had customers who would send data out to an integrator and end up with a blackbox model in a couple months at the cost of a few hundred thousand every time they did so, hoping it'd be better than the last version.

Now that it's a "strategic initiative" of pretty much every organization, I've seen a trend to rush something out the door without much understanding or validation of the implementation.  With services such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Bedrock, Vertex, etc most folks aren't bothering with even training or tuning their own models - it's much easier to justify the massive OpEx shipped off in exchange for some API keys than build a Center of Excellence around AI/ML.

I've used GitHub Copilot for years, it's been great for auto-complete sort of stuff but lately it seems that the quality has gone down.  Lately I've been a big fan of Anthropic's Claude Code - it's truely marvelous.

However you don't have to look far or wide for horror stories of Claude wiping out code bases, home directories, or shoving your queries into a Google Search that can be identified - this isn't even touching the *Claw persistent autonomous agent stacks.
